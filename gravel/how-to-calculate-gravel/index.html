How to calculate gravel

Area, depth, density, allowance: the same five steps size a garden path and a 400-ton haul road. Learn them once and every quote you receive becomes checkable.

Quick answer

How do you calculate how much gravel you need?

Multiply length × width in feet, then by depth in inches ÷ 12 for cubic feet. Divide by 27 for cubic yards and multiply by the gravel's tons per yard (1.42 for common loose gravel), then add 10% for settling. A 15 × 20 ft patio at 3 inches is 2.78 yd³, or about 4.33 tons with the allowance.

Rule of thumb:
One ton of common gravel covers about 76 ft² at 3 in deep.
Formula:
Tons = L × W × (in ÷ 12) ÷ 27 × 1.42 × 1.10

The five-step method

  1. 1

    Measure the area

    Measure length and width in feet and multiply for square feet. Split irregular shapes into rectangles and sum the pieces. Precision here is cheap, and every downstream number keys off it.

  2. 2

    Pick the depth

    2 inches for decorative top-dressing, 3 inches for paths and patios, 4 inches per lift for driveways. Depth drives cost linearly, so choose it deliberately, not by habit.

  3. 3

    Compute the volume

    Square feet × (depth in inches ÷ 12) gives cubic feet. Divide by 27 for cubic yards, the unit most suppliers quote in.

  4. 4

    Apply the type's density

    Common gravel runs 105 lb/ft³ loose (1.42 US tons per cubic yard), pea gravel 96 lb/ft³, #57 stone 100 lb/ft³ loose, crusher run 125 lb/ft³ loose. Multiply volume by density, then divide pounds by 2,000 for tons.

  5. 5

    Add a settling allowance

    Order 10% extra as standard; 15% where crusher run will be compacted or the subgrade is soft. Gravel consolidates the moment it leaves the truck, so the allowance is not optional padding.

Quote both units on the phone

Tell the dispatcher your tons and your cubic yards. If their conversion doesn't land near yours, one of you has the wrong density, and it's a thirty-second fix now versus a short or heavy load on delivery day.

Same method, three materials

Area × depth × density is the universal takeoff. The identical five steps size a concrete pour (see how to calculate concrete) and an asphalt lift, covered in how to calculate asphalt. Only the density and the waste factor change: concrete at 150 lb/ft³, hot-mix asphalt at 145 compacted, gravel at 96–140 depending on type and compaction. Master the gravel version and the other two are substitutions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula for calculating gravel in tons?
Tons = square feet × (depth in inches ÷ 12) × density (lb/ft³) ÷ 2,000, times a 1.10–1.15 waste factor. For common gravel at 105 lb/ft³, a useful shortcut is cubic yards × 1.42 = tons, because one loose cubic yard weighs about 2,800 lb.
How do I convert tons of gravel to cubic yards?
Divide tons by the material's tons per cubic yard: tons ÷ 1.42 for common loose gravel. So 10 tons of gravel is about 7 cubic yards, and 20 tons is about 14. Use ÷ 1.3 for pea gravel and ÷ 1.69 for loose crusher run, because the factor changes with the stone.
What depth should I calculate for?
2 inches for decorative cover over fabric, 3 inches for walkways and patios, and 4 inches per compacted lift for driveways; a new drive typically needs 8–12 inches total across two or three layers. Going below 2 inches leaves bare spots; the fabric shows within a season.
How many bags of gravel do I need?
Cubic feet × 2 for standard 0.5 ft³ bags, plus 10%. The 15 × 20 ft patio above is 75 ft³, so it needs about 150 bags before the allowance, which is why anything past a small path repair is cheaper to order in bulk by the ton.
How many tons is a tandem load of gravel?
About 14 tons. Divide your total tonnage by 14 for the number of tandem truck loads. In volume, 14 tons of common loose gravel is roughly 10 cubic yards (14 ÷ 1.42 = 9.9), enough for about 1,060 ft² at 3 inches deep.
Does the calculation change for crusher run or pea gravel?
Only the density changes. Pea gravel drops to 96 lb/ft³, crusher run rises to 125 lb/ft³ loose and about 140 compacted, so a crusher run order in tons runs roughly 20% heavier than common gravel for the same volume. The five steps themselves are identical for every type.
