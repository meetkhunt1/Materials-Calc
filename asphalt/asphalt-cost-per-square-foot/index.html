Asphalt cost per square foot, installed

Per-square-foot pricing is an output, not an input: it falls out of tonnage, crew time and fixed fees divided by area. That is why the same contractor quotes $3 on one street and $8 on the next.

Quick answer

How much does asphalt cost per square foot?

Installed asphalt costs about $5.85–8.10 per square foot for a 500–1,000 ft² driveway and $9.40–11.90 per square foot for jobs under 500 ft², at 3 inches compacted including excavation, base, hot mix, labor and mobilization. The hot mix itself costs $100–150 per ton and is only 40–50% of the installed price.

Rule of thumb:
Each extra compacted inch adds about $0.75–1.10 per ft².
Formula:
$/ft² = (tons × $/ton + labor + fees) ÷ area

The formula behind every bid

Asphalt cost per square foot is total job cost divided by area, so fixed fees make small jobs expensive per foot.

Material at $100–150 per ton typically accounts for 40–50% of installed cost. The rest is a 5–8 person crew, a paver and rollers, and a $1,500–3,000 mobilization minimum that lands on the job whether it is 200 square feet or 20,000. Every number below is that formula evaluated at different areas.

What thickness adds

Thickness moves the price far less than size does, because it changes only the material term: about $0.75–1.10 per square foot for each additional compacted inch, which is roughly 12 lb of mix per square foot per inch at current per-ton prices. Crew and equipment time barely move (a paver places 4 inches nearly as fast as 2), so on a job that is already mobilized, extra thickness is the cheapest structural upgrade available. Going from the 3 in residential standard to a 4 in heavy-vehicle section adds about a dollar per square foot, not a whole price tier.

Installed asphalt cost per square foot by job size

Installed asphalt costs $9.40–11.90 per square foot under 500 ft² and $5.85–8.10 per square foot at 500–1,000 ft².

Why there is no row for larger jobs

Above about 1,000 ft², the per-square-foot price keeps falling as mobilization and crew time spread across more area. How far it falls depends on access, haul distance and how the contractor schedules the work, so jobs that size are worth quoting rather than estimating from a table. The direction is certain (every job costed on this site gets cheaper per square foot as it grows), but there is no worked example here above 1,200 ft² to put a number to, and inventing one would give a guess the appearance of a measurement.

The mobilization curve

Installed cost per square foot falls from $11.30 on a 300 ft² patch to $5.85 on a 600 ft² metro job. Each bar is a job costed line by line elsewhere on this site, not a market average, which is why the curve stops at 600 square feet rather than running out to commercial lots. Below 500 square feet you are mostly renting a crew's morning, and the asphalt is almost incidental to the bill; by 600 square feet the same fixed costs are spread over twice the area, and geography starts to matter as much as size. Frequently asked questions

What does asphalt cost per square foot installed?
About $5.85–8.10 per square foot for a 500–1,000 ft² driveway and $9.40–11.90 per square foot for a job under 500 ft², all-in (excavation, base, hot mix, labor and mobilization). Job size moves the price far more than the asphalt does. Material is only 40–50% of either figure; the rest is crew, machines and the fixed cost of showing up. Working over an existing sound base removes the excavation and gravel, worth about $2.50–3.75 per square foot on a small job.
Why is my small patch quoted at $10 per square foot?
Because of mobilization. A paving contractor spends $1,500–3,000 moving crew and equipment to any job before the first ton goes down. Spread over 300 square feet, that fixed cost alone is $5–10 per square foot. The cure is combining work: do the patch when a neighbor paves, or bundle it with sealcoating and crack repair.
How much does each extra inch of thickness add?
Roughly $0.75–1.10 per square foot per inch, which is the material cost of about 12 pounds of mix per square foot per inch at 2026 per-ton prices. Labor barely changes (the paver places 4 inches nearly as fast as 2), so upgrading thickness is the cheapest structural improvement you can buy on a job that is already mobilized.
Does the price include the gravel base?
Often not, and this is the most common bid-comparison trap. Base gravel runs $18–35 per ton plus grading and compaction. On a small job that excavation-and-base scope works out to about $2.50–3.75 per square foot: on a 480 ft² driveway, $840 of excavation plus $420 of gravel. A bid that looks a dollar per square foot cheaper may simply exclude it, so make every bidder state base thickness and compaction method in writing.
Is commercial paving cheaper per square foot than residential?
Yes, materially. A big lot lets the crew run the paver continuously and spreads mobilization across far more area, while residential driveways carry hand work at the edges, small tonnage and exactly the same fixed setup. We do not publish a per-square-foot figure for large lots: Above about 1,000 ft², the per-square-foot price keeps falling as mobilization and crew time spread across more area. How far it falls depends on access, haul distance and how the contractor schedules the work, so jobs that size are worth quoting rather than estimating from a table.
How much does asphalt cost per square yard?
About $53–73 per square yard installed for a 500–1,000 ft² driveway, and $85–107 per square yard under 500 ft². A square yard is 9 ft², so multiply any per-square-foot price by 9. Commercial bids and highway pay items are often written per square yard for that reason.
