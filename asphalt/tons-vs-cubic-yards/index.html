Tons vs cubic yards

You measure the job in cubic yards; the plant sells it in tons. One factor links the two (1.96 US tons per compacted cubic yard), and getting it backwards is the most expensive unit mistake in paving.

Quick answer

How many cubic yards are in a ton of asphalt?

One US ton of compacted hot mix asphalt equals about 0.51 cubic yards, and one compacted cubic yard weighs 1.96 tons (3,915 lb), based on a density of 145 lb/ft³. So 2 tons is about 1.02 yd³, 10 tons is 5.1 yd³ and 20 tons is 10.2 yd³ of finished mat.

Rule of thumb:
A cubic yard of compacted asphalt weighs about 2 tons; a ton fills about half a yard.
Formula:
yd³ = tons ÷ 1.96 | tons = yd³ × 1.96

Two units, one material

A cubic yard measures volume and a ton measures weight, and for asphalt one compacted cubic yard weighs 1.96 tons. A cubic yard is what your tape measure produces: area times compacted thickness. A ton is what the scale house produces: the truck weighed loaded minus its tare. Compacted dense-graded hot mix weighs 145 lb/ft³, so one cubic yard of finished mat is 27 × 145 = 3,915 lb, or 1.96 US tons. Every conversion on this page is that single density restated. What that tonnage is worth in money is a separate question, and a sharper one: the asphalt driveway cost guide turns the same takeoff into an installed budget, line item by line item.

How do you convert tons to cubic yards?

Divide tons by 1.96 to get compacted cubic yards, and multiply cubic yards by 1.96 to get tons.

Loose vs compacted yards

A loose yard and a compacted yard differ by 25%

Hot mix fluffs from 145 lb/ft³ in the mat to about 117 lb/ft³ in the truck bed, so a loose cubic yard is only about 1.58 tons, not 1.96. Quotes, delivery tickets and takeoffs that say "yards" without stating the condition are ambiguous by a quarter of the material. Always state which you mean; when in doubt, work in tons, which never change.

The same 25% gap is why thickness has to be specified as compacted depth before anyone converts anything. On a driveway, the choice between 2, 3 and 4 inches is the one that decides both the tonnage and the lifespan. The right asphalt thickness for a driveway matches the section to the vehicles that will park on it.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons is a cubic yard of asphalt?
One cubic yard of compacted asphalt weighs 1.96 US tons: 27 ft³ × 145 lb/ft³ = 3,915 lb. Rounding to 2 tons per yard overstates the order by about 2%, which is usually harmless and often useful as built-in waste. Loose material in the truck is only about 1.58 tons per cubic yard.
How many cubic yards is a ton of asphalt?
One ton of asphalt is 0.51 compacted cubic yards (1 ton ÷ 1.96 tons/yd³). In coverage terms, a ton placed 3 inches thick compacted covers about 53 ft².
Is a ton more than a cubic yard?
For asphalt, a cubic yard is more than a ton: one compacted cubic yard weighs 1.96 tons, so a ton fills only about half a yard. The two are different kinds of unit (a yard measures space, a ton measures weight), so the comparison only works once you know the material's density.
How many yards is 2 tons of asphalt?
2 tons of asphalt is about 1.02 compacted cubic yards. That is enough to pave roughly 106 ft² at 3 inches compacted, which suits a pothole run or a small walkway patch.
How many yards is 20 tons of asphalt?
20 tons of asphalt is about 10.2 compacted cubic yards (20 ÷ 1.96). Twenty tons is a typical tandem-plus truckload, and at 3 inches compacted it paves about 1,060 ft².
Why do asphalt plants sell by the ton instead of by the yard?
Plants sell by the ton because weight is unambiguous. Volume changes with temperature, handling and compaction (the same ton occupies about 25% more space loose than rolled), but the scale ticket reads the same regardless. Plants batch by weight, trucks are weighed loaded and empty, and you pay for the difference.
Should I convert loose or compacted yards to tons?
Convert compacted yards, almost always. Your takeoff (area × thickness) describes the finished, rolled mat, so multiply by 1.96 tons/yd³. Only use the loose factor (about 1.58 tons/yd³) when you are sizing truck volume or estimating a stockpile, never for ordering against plan dimensions.
How much extra should I add when converting a takeoff to an order?
Add 5% for handbook waste on straightforward geometry: handwork around structures, yield variation and cleanup. Irregular edges, many penetrations or thin variable-depth leveling courses justify 7–10%. Then round up to the plant's sale increment, typically the half ton or full ton.
Does the 1.96 factor change with mix type?
Yes, a little. It is built on 145 lb/ft³ dense-graded hot mix; cold mix compacts nearer 137 lb/ft³ (1.85 tons/yd³) and compacted millings about 122 lb/ft³ (1.65 tons/yd³). If the mix design sheet lists a different maximum density, derive the factor from that instead of the table value.
