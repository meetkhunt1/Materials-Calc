Quick answer

How much does asphalt cost?

Hot mix asphalt costs about $100–150 per ton at the plant in 2026, before trucking. Installed at 3 inches compacted, including excavation, base, labor and mobilization, a 500–1,000 ft² driveway costs $5.85–8.10 per square foot. Smaller jobs cost more per square foot because a $1,500–3,000 mobilization fee is spread over less area.

Rule of thumb:
Material is 40–50% of an installed asphalt job; the rest is crew, equipment, trucking and fees.

Distance is a hidden spec

Hot mix must arrive above about 250°F to compact properly. Past roughly 45–60 minutes of haul, plants either surcharge, insulate, or decline the job, which is why the nearest plant effectively sets your price, and rural jobs quote high regardless of tonnage.

Thickness drives the installed price

Each extra inch adds roughly $0.90–1.00/ft²: pure material and rolling time, since the crew and mobilization are already on site. This is why upgrading from 3 in to 4 in costs far less than the 33% the thickness suggests, and why skimping an inch saves less than people hope.

Frequently asked questions

How much does asphalt cost per ton in 2026?
Hot mix runs $100–150 per ton at the plant, with commodity swings tracking the price of PG binder, the oil-derived glue that is only 5–6% of the mix by weight but a third of its cost. Cold patch in bags costs several times more per ton; millings a fraction. Pickup price excludes trucking.
How much does asphalt paving cost per square foot?
About $5.85–8.10 per square foot for a 500–1,000 ft² driveway and $9.40–11.90 per square foot for a job under 500 ft², all-in (excavation, base, hot mix, labor and mobilization). Job size drives it far more than most tables admit. Material is only 40–50% of either; the rest is trucking, crew, paver and roller time. Above 1,000 ft² we do not publish a rate: larger jobs keep getting cheaper per square foot as fixed costs spread, but the drop depends on site access and haul distance, so get them quoted.
Why do small asphalt jobs cost so much per square foot?
Because of fixed costs. Moving a paver, roller and crew to your site costs $1,500 or more before the first ton hits the ground, and plants charge minimum-load fees on partial trucks. Spread $1,500 over 600 ft² and mobilization alone adds $2.50/ft², which is why patching one driveway costs double the per-foot rate of paving a whole street.
What share of an asphalt job is the material?
Typically 40–50% of the installed price on residential work. A 600 ft² driveway at 3 in needs about 11.5 tons; at $125/ton that is $1,440 of mix on a job quoting around $3,000–3,300. The balance is labor, equipment, trucking and base work. On large commercial tonnage, material share rises past 60%.
Is it cheaper to DIY an asphalt driveway?
No, and mostly it is not possible. Hot mix arrives at 300°F and must be laid and rolled within a tight temperature window by a paver and a multi-ton roller, equipment you cannot meaningfully rent for a weekend. DIY territory is cold-patch repairs and sealcoating. For new pavement, competitive quotes are the cost lever, not your labor.
How do asphalt and concrete driveway costs compare?
Asphalt is cheaper to install: a 500–1,000 ft² asphalt driveway runs $5.85–8.10 per square foot all-in. We are re-sourcing our concrete per-square-foot figures, so we are not quoting a concrete rate until we have one we can stand behind. What is not in doubt is the shape of the trade: asphalt costs less on day one, then wants sealcoating every 3–5 years and an overlay around year 15–20, while concrete costs more up front and asks for very little afterwards.
