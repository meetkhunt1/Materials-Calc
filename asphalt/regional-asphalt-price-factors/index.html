Quick answer

Why do asphalt prices vary by region?

Asphalt prices vary by up to about 40% between regions because installed cost depends on local factors: distance to the nearest hot mix plant (a practical haul radius is about 25 miles), binder freight, paving season length, crew labor rates and contractor competition. The asphalt itself varies far less than trucking and labor do.

Rule of thumb:
Distance to the plant moves the price more than which state you are in.

How much does asphalt cost by US region?

The Northeast and West Coast run highest and the South lowest, with the Mountain West showing the widest spread.

The regional index at a glance

What actually drives the spread

Six factors explain nearly all regional variation. Plant proximity first: hot mix has a practical haul radius of about 25 miles before temperature loss compromises compaction, so your price is set by the plants inside that circle. One plant is a monopoly; five is a market. Binder terminal distance adds silent freight on the costliest ingredient. Aggregate availability matters where good stone is scarce and quarried material travels. Season length compresses demand in the north. Labor markets set crew rates that vary nearly two-to-one across states. And competition density (contractors per capita) determines how much of all that cost structure gets passed through versus absorbed.

Note what is not on the list: the asphalt itself. Per-ton plant pricing varies far less than installed pricing (see the price per ton guide) because binder is priced off national indices. The regional spread lives in trucking, labor, season and competition, which is why it shows up in installed quotes more than in material quotes.

Short season, compressed demand

A plant that closes from November to April sells a year of tonnage in six or seven months, and every contractor's calendar fills accordingly. Northern owners pay for that scarcity twice: higher unit prices, and less scheduling leverage, because the mid-season discount window that southern owners enjoy barely exists. If you are paving in a cold state, book early and be flexible on dates; flexibility is the only discount lever the calendar leaves you.

Frequently asked questions

Which US region has the cheapest asphalt?
The South generally has the cheapest asphalt, thanks to year-round paving seasons, dense plant networks, competitive labor markets and strong contractor competition. We do not publish a per-square-foot figure by region, because region turns out to be a blunt instrument: the worked example below puts the same 600 ft² driveway at $3,500 in a competitive metro and $4,845 at a rural site 40 miles from the nearest plant. Distance to the plant and the crew moves the price more than which state you are in.
Why does distance from the plant matter so much?
Distance matters because hot mix leaves the plant near 300°F and must be placed and compacted before it cools too far, which sets a practical haul radius of about 25 miles, or roughly 45–60 minutes. Beyond that, contractors pay for insulated trucks, warm-mix additives or a more distant plant premium, and trucking itself adds $8–20 per ton before any of that.
Why is asphalt more expensive in northern states?
Asphalt costs more in northern states because of season compression. Plants in cold states shut down for four to six months, so a year of demand squeezes into six to eight months of capacity, and contractors price the backlog. Add prevailing higher labor costs and freeze-thaw base requirements (deeper gravel, more excavation) and installed prices run 15–25% above the national average.
Do binder terminals affect my local price?
Yes, though invisibly. Liquid binder moves from refineries to regional terminals by barge, rail and pipeline; plants far from a terminal pay meaningful freight on a $600–700-per-ton input that is 5–6% of every ton of mix. It is one reason inland and mountain markets show wider, less predictable pricing than coastal and river-corridor markets.
How do I use regional data when reading a quote?
Treat it as a sanity band, not a target. If your quotes cluster inside the regional range, the market is working; if a single quote sits far above, ask what scope explains it (base work, removal, access) before assuming gouging. If all quotes sit high, your micro-market (plant distance, few contractors) is the cause, and more bids from farther afield may help.
