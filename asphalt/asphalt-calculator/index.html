Quick answer

How much asphalt do I need?

Multiply the area in square feet by the compacted thickness in feet, then by 145 lb/ft³ for hot mix asphalt, and divide by 2,000 to get US tons. A 10×10 ft area at 3 inches compacted needs about 1.99 tons including 10% waste, and one cubic yard of compacted hot mix weighs about 1.96 tons.

Rule of thumb:
One ton of hot mix asphalt covers about 80 ft² at 2 in, or 53 ft² at 3 in, compacted.
Formula:
Tons = Area × depth × 145 lb/ft³ ÷ 2,000 × (1 + waste)

How do you calculate tons of asphalt?

Tons of asphalt equal area times compacted thickness times 145 lb/ft³, divided by 2,000. The one asphalt-specific twist versus a concrete takeoff: the answer converts to weight, because plants batch and bill by the certified scale ticket. Everything upstream (measuring, unit conversion, waste) is the same discipline. If you only need the volume, the volume calculator skips the density step.

Worked example: parking pad

A 30 × 18 ft pad at 4 inches needs about 14 tons, and every line of that example depends on the area being right. Areas that are not simple rectangles (curved aprons, flares, irregular lots) are handled in the asphalt measurement guide, and if the job is a driveway, the standard driveway dimensions give workable widths and turnaround sizes before you measure anything at all.

Mistakes that blow asphalt orders

Four ways tonnage estimates go wrong

1. Using loose thickness instead of compacted, a 25% overstatement. 2. Assuming concrete's density (150 lb/ft³ vs 145) when converting old tables. 3. Forgetting that overlays need a leveling course on rutted pavement, which adds 10–20% tonnage that never shows in plan math. 4. Ordering exact: hot mix can't wait for a second truck, because it's workable for roughly 2–3 hours after loading, less in cold wind.

Verify with the delivery ticket

Every load comes with a scale ticket showing net tons. Track tickets against this estimate during the pour. If you're at 60% of area but 75% of tonnage, the crew is running thick and you'll be a truck short by the end.

A tonnage figure only becomes a budget once it meets a price, and the questions that follow (what a ton should cost, what drives quotes apart, what is negotiable) are answered in the asphalt cost FAQs. To sanity-check the order from the other direction, the asphalt coverage guide states what one ton actually covers at each thickness, which catches an order that is off by a factor before the trucks are booked.

Frequently asked questions

How many tons of asphalt do I need?
Multiply area (ft²) by compacted thickness (ft) to get cubic feet, then by 145 lb/ft³, then divide by 2,000. Shortcut: one ton of hot mix covers about 80 ft² at 2 inches, and coverage halves each time you double the thickness.
How do I sanity-check my tonnage with coverage?
Divide your area by the coverage per ton at your compacted thickness: 160 ft² at 1 in, 80 ft² at 2 in, 53 ft² at 3 in, 40 ft² at 4 in. The coverage guide has the full table with metric equivalents.
Do I measure loose or compacted thickness?
Plans and this calculator use compacted (after rolling) thickness. Crews spread roughly 25% thicker to compensate, so a 3 in compacted lift is laid at about 3¾ in loose. Order from compacted numbers; the paver handles the fluff factor.
Why does asphalt sell by the ton and not by the yard?
Every truck crosses a certified scale at the plant, so weight is exact and auditable; volume changes with temperature and compaction. Tickets, quotes and pay items are all in tons. The tons vs cubic yards guide covers converting between them.
What thickness should I use?
Residential driveways: 3 in total (two lifts) over 6 in of base. Parking lots: 4 in+. Overlays on sound pavement: 1.5–2 in. Highways and truck yards are engineered per layer; the thickness guide has the full breakdown.
Does the calculator work for cold patch?
Yes. Select cold mix in the mix dropdown. Note that bagged cold patch is sold by the 50 lb bag: divide the pound result by 50 for a bag count on pothole repairs.
How many tons of asphalt are in a cubic yard?
One cubic yard of compacted hot mix asphalt weighs about 1.96 tons. That comes from 27 ft³ × 145 lb/ft³ = 3,915 lb, divided by 2,000. Going the other way, one ton is about 0.51 cubic yards.
How do I convert square yards to tons of asphalt?
Multiply square yards by compacted thickness in inches, then by 110 lb, and divide by 2,000. One square yard at 1 inch is 9 ft² × (1 ÷ 12) ft × 145 lb/ft³ ≈ 110 lb (the paver's 110 rule), so 100 yd² at 2 inches needs about 11 tons before waste.
