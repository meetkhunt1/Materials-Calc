Concrete mix ratios, decoded

1:2:4 and its siblings are volumetric recipes older than ready-mix, and they are still how much of the world batches concrete. Here's what the numbers mean and when each mix is appropriate.

Quick answer

What is the standard concrete mix ratio?

The standard concrete mix ratio is 1:2:4 by volume: 1 part cement, 2 parts sand and 4 parts 20 mm stone, with a water-cement ratio near 0.5. It gives about 2,200 psi (M15, roughly C15) for paths and unreinforced slabs. Anything with rebar, including driveways and footings, needs 1:1.5:3 (M20, about 2,900 psi) or richer.

Rule of thumb:
Rebar in the pour means 1:1.5:3 (M20) or richer.
Formula:
w/c = water weight ÷ cement weight (target 0.45–0.55)

One rule covers most decisions

Anything containing rebar gets M20 (1:1.5:3) or better. That's the IS 456 floor for reinforced work, and it maps closely to the 3,000 psi minimum US codes expect for exterior flatwork.

What does a 1:2:4 mix ratio mean?

A 1:2:4 mix means 1 part cement, 2 parts sand and 4 parts coarse aggregate, measured by volume in that order. One bucket of cement, two of sand, four of 20 mm stone. The sand fills voids between stones; the cement paste coats everything and glues it. Roughly speaking, the finished concrete volume is about two-thirds of the summed loose ingredient volumes, because sand and paste disappear into the voids. That is why batch tables, not intuition, size the ingredient piles.

Water-cement ratio: the strength dial

Water, not cement, decides the final strength of a given mix: keep the water-cement ratio between 0.45 and 0.55 for site work.

Water is where site mixes fail

Crews add water because wet concrete places easily, but every extra liter permanently dilutes the paste. A 1:1.5:3 mix at w/c 0.7 tests weaker than a 1:2:4 at 0.5. Fix workability with grading and admixtures, or accept a stiffer mix and better compaction, but hold the water line. Strength gain over time is covered in the curing guide.

Batching quantities per cubic meter

One cubic meter of 1:2:4 concrete takes about 6.3 bags of 50 kg cement, 0.44 m³ of sand and 0.88 m³ of aggregate. Richer mixes need more cement per cubic meter.

Frequently asked questions

What is the strongest concrete mix ratio?
1:1:2 (about M25) is the richest nominal mix in common use. Beyond that, you don't get stronger concrete by adding cement. You get it by lowering the water-cement ratio and using admixtures, which is design-mix territory (IS 10262 / ACI 211).
What is the C15, C20 and C40 concrete mix ratio?
C15 is roughly a 1:2:4 mix and C20 is roughly 1:1.5:3, matching the M15 and M20 rows in the chart above. C40 has no reliable nominal ratio: it sits well above the richest volumetric mix (1:1:2, about M25), so it has to be a design mix with a low water-cement ratio, usually ordered as ready-mix.
What concrete mix ratio should I use for a slab?
Use 1:1.5:3 (M20, about 2,900 psi) for any slab with rebar or mesh, including driveways and garage floors. A plain 1:2:4 mix is fine for unreinforced paths and shed bases. Keep the water-cement ratio between 0.45 and 0.55 either way.
Is 1:2:3 a good concrete mix ratio?
Yes, 1:2:3 is a reasonable general-purpose mix. It sits between the 1:1.5:3 (M20) and 1:2:4 (M15) rows: a little richer than 1:2:4 because it carries less coarse aggregate per bag of cement. For reinforced work, 1:1.5:3 is still the safer choice.
What mix ratio gives 4,000 or 5,000 psi concrete?
No nominal site mix reliably reaches 4,000 or 5,000 psi. The richest common ratio, 1:1:2, tests around 3,600 psi when site-cured. Higher strengths come from a design mix with a lower water-cement ratio, which is why bagged 4,000 psi mixes and ready-mix orders specify strength rather than a ratio.
What is the ratio for general-purpose concrete?
1:2:4 by volume (cement : sand : 20 mm aggregate) with a 0.5–0.55 water-cement ratio. That gives roughly M15 (about C15) and 2,200 psi as batched on site. For driveways and structural work, step up to 1:1.5:3 (M20, about 2,900 psi).
How much water per bag of cement?
At w/c 0.50, a 50 kg cement bag takes 25 liters (a 94 lb US bag takes about 21 quarts). Count the water already in damp sand: it's commonly 3–5% by weight and pushes your real w/c well above target if ignored.
Why do bagged mixes not list a ratio?
Bagged concrete is pre-blended to a target strength (typically 4,000 psi at proper water content); the ratio decision has been made for you. Ratios matter when batching from separate cement, sand and aggregate.
Can I use a 1:3:6 mix for anything structural?
No. 1:3:6 (≈M10) is for blinding layers, fill and mass concrete without reinforcement. IS 456 sets M20 as the minimum grade for reinforced concrete; using leaner mixes around rebar invites corrosion and cracking.

Get the volume before the ratio

Batch tables key off cubic meters, so measure the pour first.
