Quick answer

What is the density of concrete?

Normal-weight reinforced concrete has a density of about 2,400 kg/m³ (150 lb/ft³, or 24 kN/m³). Plain, unreinforced concrete is about 2,300 kg/m³ (145 lb/ft³), and fresh wet concrete runs slightly heavier at about 2,450 kg/m³. A cubic yard of cured concrete weighs roughly 4,050 lb, just over two US tons.

Rule of thumb:
Use 2,400 kg/m³ (150 lb/ft³) unless a mix design sheet says otherwise.
Formula:
Weight = Volume × Density

Density of concrete by type

Normal-weight concrete sits at 2,300–2,400 kg/m³. Lightweight mixes drop as low as 600 kg/m³ and heavyweight shielding concrete reaches about 3,800 kg/m³.

Sand bulking will wreck volume batching

Damp sand occupies up to a quarter more volume than the same mass of dry sand. A 1:2:4 mix batched with damp-sand buckets is silently cement-rich on sand and gravel-poor, which is one reason site-mixed strength varies so much. Weigh batching (or at least a bulking correction) fixes it; details in the mix ratio guide.

Density in the volume-to-weight formula

Multiply the pour volume by the density to get its weight: 1 m³ of reinforced concrete weighs about 2,400 kg.

Where the weight number matters

Formwork pressure, crane picks, transport loads and dead-load takedowns all start from this multiplication. It's also the fastest sanity check on a delivery ticket: yards × 4,050 lb should roughly match the batch weights printed on it.

Which density should you design with?

For estimating deliveries and dead loads on ordinary work, use 2,400 kg/m³ (150 lb/ft³). That is the reinforced normal-weight value that ACI 318 and IS 456 both build their load tables around. Use the plain-concrete 2,300 figure only for unreinforced mass pours, and switch to measured unit weight (ASTM C138) whenever a mix design sheet is available, because specialty aggregates move the number more than any table can capture.

Density questions

What is the density of concrete?
Normal-weight concrete is 2,300–2,400 kg/m³ (145–150 lb/ft³). Design codes use 145 lb/ft³ for plain and 150 lb/ft³ for reinforced concrete (ACI 318); IS 456 uses 24 kN/m³ ≈ 2,400 kg/m³ for RCC.
How many tons is a yard of concrete?
About 2 US tons. A cubic yard of normal-weight concrete weighs roughly 4,050 lb, just over two short tons. A cubic meter weighs about 2,400 kg. A full 10-yard mixer truck therefore carries over 40,000 lb of concrete alone, which is why trucks stay off finished driveways and septic fields.
Is wet concrete heavier than dry?
Yes, slightly. Fresh concrete carries mix water that later evaporates or binds chemically, so it weighs roughly 50–100 kg/m³ more than the cured slab. For formwork design you use the wet (fluid) weight; for structural dead load, the cured weight.
Why does lightweight concrete exist?
Lightweight concrete cuts dead load. Replacing gravel with expanded shale or clay drops density to about 1,750 kg/m³ while keeping structural strength (ASTM C330). On a high-rise floor slab, that 25% dead-load reduction cascades into smaller beams, columns and footings, which is worth the higher material price.
What is the unit weight of concrete in kN/m³?
Reinforced concrete has a unit weight of about 24 kN/m³, the value IS 456 uses for RCC dead loads. Plain concrete at 2,300 kg/m³ works out to about 22.6 kN/m³ (2,300 × 9.81 ÷ 1,000).
What is the density of cement?
Loose bulk Portland cement has a density of about 1,440 kg/m³ (90 lb/ft³). That is why a 94 lb US bag holds roughly one cubic foot. It is far lighter than finished concrete because the bulk powder is full of air.
What concrete is used for radiation shielding?
Heavyweight concrete batched with magnetite, barite or steel punchings reaches 3,200–4,000 kg/m³. Density is the shielding mechanism, so these mixes are specified by required mass per square meter of wall.
